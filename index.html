Touchline · the season scorebook

Every player, every score, every match, all free

Touchline gathers what 1,012 teams publish on their schools' own websites and keeps the current season in one place, up to date on a phone between classes or a laptop in the press box.

If it doesn't work on a phone in the stands, it doesn't ship. —Ed.

  • Scores, standings, player sheets and the championship bracket, with a written line on every conference.
  • Gaps stay visible. A match that hasn't been reported shows as missing, not guessed.
  • AI writes the journal, code serves the page. Each morning, a model rewrites the journal for any conference whose data changed: the headline, findings and players to watch. A validator checks each finding that has a checkable basis against the data, drops any the data contradicts, and flags for review any headline number that isn't in the data behind it. The stats are plain code on a static site, so pages load fast, cost very little to serve and still work if a journal is missing.

  • The whole archive on your computer: box scores for men and women in Division I and II since 2022, and two Division II men's conferences back to 2016.
  • Bring your own AI. Connect an account you already have and pick the model. Desk never sees a password.
  • A number that doesn't recount is cut. Every figure in an answer has to be found again in the record it came from; a sentence whose figure isn't is removed, not softened.
  • Works without AI, too. Pages, search, favorites and the published news need no AI account.

Runnable labs

One Python lab per module, offline, on an invented conference shaped like the real data. Everyone gets the same output, and one command runs every lab and checks each result.

Learning with AI, on purpose

In Module 1, "Ask an AI" exercises turn a concept into questions for an assistant, such as whether a collection is complete or why a blank is not a zero, and then show you how to check the answer.

It opens with a story

Engineers who can check the AI's work

AI writes much of the code now. What's scarce is the person who knows why a collection is incomplete or a blank isn't a zero. Learn teaches that judgment on a real system, in the open, which is also how we show our numbers deserve trust.
